What makes first-time investors stop their SIP?

This engine read 38,963 public conversations about SIPs and mutual funds (app reviews, YouTube comments, Reddit and TradingQ&A) and tagged each one against a fixed codebook of 19 questions. The tags were then linked to the metric: the share of first-time investors with an active SIP 12 months after starting.

How the engine works

Six steps, run in Google Colab on the full corpus, with a human checkpoint at each one. AI is used only where reading meaning is needed (steps 3 and 4); everything else is fixed code, so it is cheap, repeatable and auditable.

The steps

  1. Collect. Play Store reviews for Groww and 5 competitors from 1 Jan 2023 (about 295,000), App Store reviews, 59,394 comments from 93 personal finance videos, 9,752 TradingQ&A posts and 1,406 Reddit items. Public data only; usernames are never used. X and Instagram were skipped (paid access and platform terms).
  2. Content check (code). Removes empty and generic items, and labels replies prompted by a creator's call to action. Excluded items keep a reason; nothing is deleted.
  3. Relevance (AI, Claude Haiku 5.5). Five models were tested on 100 hand-labelled items; Haiku won on recall within budget. A blind audit afterwards put recall at about 80% (range 67 to 91%).
  4. Tag with the codebook (AI). 19 fixed questions: kind of SIP, first-time or not, why they started, what they expected, what they did, what triggered it, what worried them, what they check, where they go for help, what they wish for, and segment signals. The AI must pick from fixed options and quote the words behind every tag. Prompt v1.5, refined five times on a practice set.
  5. Derive (code). Fixed rules turn tags into the metric view: scope (mutual fund SIP or not), whose experience it is, where it sits in the metric tree, a severity tier and an opportunity path.
  6. Quantify and rank (code plus judgement). Shares are counted per source, never pooled. Opportunities pass four gates, then sit on a frequency by severity grid. Findings were then tested in 4 user interviews.

Doubt is the most common experience, but cash collisions are what break the SIP

Read on the analysis set: 20,146 mutual fund items, of which 12,257 describe the writer's own experience or someone they know. Frequency is a theme's rank within each source; severity is the share of that theme's items that ended in a lapse or a missed instalment.

Target chosen: the first tight month in a young salaried first-timer's first year, when the SIP date collides with a cash shortfall. The engine shows it is common and severe; interviews showed why: the break is temporary and cash-driven, yet it counts as a lapse.
